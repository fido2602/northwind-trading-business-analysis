
# Northwind Trading | Business Analysis Case Study

## Project Overview

This project demonstrates a structured Business Analysis approach to investigating a decline in sales for a fictional retail business, **Northwind Trading**.

The business experienced a significant decrease in sales from February to March, despite an increase in website visits. The objective was to investigate the underlying business problem, analyze customer complaints, identify potential root causes, assess stakeholder needs, and recommend practical improvements.

The case study follows a business-focused approach, combining quantitative analysis, qualitative analysis, root cause investigation, stakeholder management, and project planning.

**Project Type:** Business Analysis Case Study  
**Industry:** Retail / E-commerce  
**Tools & Techniques:** Excel, Quantitative Analysis, Qualitative Analysis, Five Whys, Stakeholder Analysis, Power–Interest Grid, RACI Matrix  
**Project Context:** Microsoft Business Analyst learning exercise

---

## Business Problem

Northwind Trading reported declining sales in March compared with February, even though website traffic increased.

This raised an important business question:

**Why were sales declining while website visits were increasing?**

The investigation focused on identifying possible issues in the customer purchasing experience, particularly website usability and the checkout process.

---

## Key Findings

### 1. Quantitative Analysis

A comparison of February and March performance revealed the following:

| KPI | February | March |
|---|---:|---:|
| Total Sales | $71,626 | $43,900 |
| Transactions | 477 | 323 |
| Average Transaction Value | $150.16 | $135.91 |
| Website Visits | 3,136 | 3,263 |

**Key observations:**

- Sales declined by approximately 38.71%.
- Transactions decreased by approximately 32.29%.
- Average transaction value declined by approximately 9.49%.
- Website visits increased by approximately 4.05%.

The findings suggested that increased website traffic was not translating into purchases at the same rate.

The transaction-to-visit ratio also declined from approximately 15.21% to 9.90%. This was treated as a preliminary conversion proxy rather than a validated conversion rate.

### 2. Qualitative Analysis

Customer complaints were reviewed and categorized to identify recurring issues.

A total of 15 complaints were analyzed.

| Complaint Category | Complaints | Share |
|---|---:|---:|
| Checkout Complexity / Usability | 5 | 33.3% |
| Website Redesign / Visual Layout | 3 | 20.0% |
| Mandatory Registration | 3 | 20.0% |
| Limited Payment Options | 1 | 6.7% |
| Navigation / Product Discovery | 1 | 6.7% |
| Website Speed / Performance | 1 | 6.7% |
| Product Information Quality | 1 | 6.7% |

The most frequent concerns involved checkout complexity, mandatory registration, and website layout.

These findings highlighted potential friction points in the customer purchasing journey.

---

## Root Cause Analysis: Five Whys

The Five Whys technique was used to investigate why customers might be abandoning purchases.

The working diagnosis focused on:

1. Customers experienced difficulties during checkout.
2. The purchasing process required registration before completing an order.
3. Unnecessary information requirements may have increased friction.
4. Website changes may not have been sufficiently evaluated from the customer perspective.
5. Usability testing and customer feedback may not have been adequately incorporated before implementation.

**Working root cause hypothesis:** Checkout friction, particularly mandatory registration and unnecessary information requirements, may have contributed to customer abandonment and declining transactions.

This is a hypothesis based on the available case-study evidence, not a proven causal conclusion.

---

## User and Stakeholder Analysis

The investigation considered the needs of different users and stakeholders involved in the proposed website improvement.

Key stakeholder perspectives included:

- **Customers:** Want a simple, convenient, and reliable purchasing experience.
- **Sales Manager:** Prioritizes reducing checkout abandonment and improving sales.
- **Marketing Manager:** Values customer registration and access to customer information for marketing activities.
- **Project Manager:** Coordinates requirements, timelines, resources, and project delivery.
- **Development Team:** Needs clear requirements and prioritized technical tasks.

### Stakeholder Conflict

A potential conflict existed between the Sales Manager's preference for guest checkout and the Marketing Manager's preference for mandatory registration.

A proposed compromise was to introduce guest checkout while keeping account registration optional, with transparent customer consent for data collection.

## Business Recommendations

Based on the findings, the following improvements were proposed:

1. Introduce guest checkout to reduce barriers to purchasing.
2. Simplify the checkout process and remove unnecessary required fields.
3. Expand available payment options.
4. Improve website navigation and product discovery.
5. Review website loading speed and visual layout.
6. Improve product descriptions and information clarity.
7. Conduct usability testing before releasing website changes.
8. Collect and review customer feedback regularly.

These recommendations aim to improve the customer experience while supporting the business objectives of increasing completed transactions and sales.

## Proposed Success Measures

The effectiveness of the proposed improvements could be evaluated using:

- Sales revenue
- Number of completed transactions
- Website conversion rate
- Checkout abandonment rate
- Average transaction value
- Website loading performance
- Customer complaints
- Customer satisfaction and feedback

These measures would help determine whether the changes produce measurable improvements.

## Project Planning and Responsibilities

A proposed delivery approach included:

1. Identify website usability issues.
2. Gather and prioritize stakeholder requirements.
3. Redesign the checkout experience.
4. Conduct usability testing.
5. Develop the website improvements.
6. Launch the updated website.
7. Monitor website performance and customer feedback.
8. Evaluate the effectiveness of the changes.

A RACI Matrix was used to clarify responsibilities and accountability across project activities.

## Project Limitations

This is a fictional, course-based business scenario.

The analysis is based on the information and data provided in the learning exercise. The relationship between website usability issues and declining sales is a working hypothesis and would require further validation using website analytics, checkout funnel data, usability testing, and customer research.

The recommendations are proposed improvements. They have not been implemented or measured in a live business environment.
